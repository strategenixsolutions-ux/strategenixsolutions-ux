## What I actually do

- Convert manual enterprise workflows into reusable AI skills, starting from a step-by-step walkthrough of how the team works today rather than an assumption about it.
- Apply a build-versus-run test to every automation candidate. Where a process is high volume and deterministic, have the model generate a purpose-built application once instead of paying inference on every run.
- Stand up the governance that makes AI adoption hold: intake gates, release discipline, and verification by independent read-back rather than by an agent's self-report.
- Run portfolio health reads that compute an independent signal from how work behaves, then reconcile it against what owners report, so a leadership review opens on the disagreement instead of on a slide.

## What this is not

I do not train models, own an MLOps platform, or ship production software as a product engineer. What I do is design, govern and run the delivery system that agent work lives inside, and prove it holds up under audit. The repositories above are deliberately small and readable for the same reason: the claim is about method, and a method you cannot run is an assertion.

I have also not published a figure for how much of a delivery cycle my agent teams automate. Agents handle the mechanical layer well. Priority, sequencing, stakeholder nuance and whether a commitment made in a meeting is the right commitment are not reliable, and treating them as reliable is how programs get hurt.
